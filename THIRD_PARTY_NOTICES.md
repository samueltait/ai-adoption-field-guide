## Referenced frameworks

**Australia's Guidance for AI Adoption**
National AI Centre, Department of Industry, Science and Resources.
Crown copyright. The six essential practices are used as the organising
structure of the tier model. The guide links to the official source and does
not reproduce its text.

**NIST AI Risk Management Framework 1.0**
National Institute of Standards and Technology, US Department of Commerce.
A US Government work. The four core functions are mapped to each tier. The
guide links to the official source and does not reproduce its text.

**DTA Policy for the responsible use of AI in government (v2.0)**
Digital Transformation Agency, Australian Government. Crown copyright.
Referenced only to note that impact assessments are mandatory for in-scope
Commonwealth agency use cases.

## Adapted and cited work

**Paweł Huryn, "The Intent Engineering Framework for AI Agents"**
*Product Compass*, 13 January 2026. Rights reserved by the author; no licence
is stated on the article. The idea of grading AI decision authority in tiers
comes from this work. Huryn's level names and his seven-part specification are
his published work and are not used in this guide. See REFERENCES.md [4].

**Simon Spencer and Edgelabs, "The Conscience Graph: a built-in, layered,
provenanced value graph for AI reasoning"**
Version 0.2, September 2026. Paper and diagrams CC BY 4.0; schema and
reference code Apache 2.0. Four mechanisms are adapted in this guide, each
marked in place with the changes made and attributed to Simon Spencer and
Edgelabs, naming the Conscience Graph as the source concept. This guide does
not claim origination of the concept, the layered model, the four-band fast
path, re-attribution, or qualification-bound delegation caps. Passages adapted
from the paper may be reused under CC BY 4.0 with attribution to Spencer and
Edgelabs as well as to this guide. See REFERENCES.md [5].

**Saleh Afroogh, Kush R. Varshney and Jason D'Cruz, "A Task-Driven
Human-AI Collaboration: When to Automate, When to Collaborate, When to
Challenge"**
arXiv:2505.18422 [cs.CY], first posted 23 May 2025. Rights reserved by the
authors. Cited in this guide to mark the boundary between task-level
allocation and use-case-level tiering. The risk by complexity matrix, the
three AI roles and the agency triad are the authors' framework. Nothing from
the paper is reproduced or adapted here. See REFERENCES.md [10].
