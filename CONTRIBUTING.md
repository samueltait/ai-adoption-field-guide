# Contributing

**Author:** Samuel Tait  
**Licence:** CC BY 4.0 (see [LICENSE](LICENSE))

Corrections, questions and suggestions are welcome. This is a governance reference maintained by one practitioner, so contributions are reviewed for fit with the guide's scope and structure rather than merged automatically.

## How to contribute

- **Report an error or unclear passage:** open an issue that quotes the passage, says what is wrong or unclear, and, where possible, cites a primary source.
- **Propose a change:** open an issue first describing the change and why. A pull request is welcome once the change is agreed.
- **Add a worked example:** examples must be fictional, must not resemble a real organisation, and must show the full chain: inputs, proposed AI role, tier factors, entry gates, provisional tier, decision owners, oversight patterns, stop rules, measures, and a promotion or review decision.

## What will not be accepted

- Client material, real organisation detail, or anything that could be traced to a real engagement.
- Statistics from consulting reports, analyst notes or news articles without a primary source.
- Text copied from Australia's Guidance for AI Adoption, the NIST AI RMF or any other referenced work. Link to them; do not reproduce them.
- Changes that turn the guide into legal advice, a certification scheme or a compliance checklist for a specific law.
- Code, GitHub Actions, badges, widgets or binary files. The guide is plain Markdown so that it can be read and edited anywhere.

## Style

- Plain Markdown; no HTML.
- Australian English.
- No em dashes; use colons, commas, parentheses or a new sentence.
- Every substantive section must produce something the reader can use: a tier, a named role, a stop rule, a measure.
- Cite sources in [REFERENCES.md](REFERENCES.md) with title, publisher, date, URL and access date.
